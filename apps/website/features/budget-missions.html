Budget MissionsMission previews

Your life stage. Your next money lesson.

From your first paycheck to retirement, practice everyday decisions through simple money missions.

14–17 First paycheck

The Shoes or the Rest of Your Week?

You worked hard for that $250. The shoes you’ve wanted cost $200. But you still need lunch money and a ride to work. Can you get what you want without leaving yourself stuck?

18–24 Independent living

Rent Paid. Fridge Empty. Now What?

Your first place finally feels like freedom—until rent takes most of your paycheck. The fridge is empty, the lights cost money, and payday is two weeks away. How will you make this work?

25–34 Variable income

Your Hours Got Cut. Your Bills Didn’t.

You open your paycheck and your stomach drops. Fewer shifts mean less money but rent, groceries, and the car payment are still due. What can you change before something falls behind?

35–44 Family expenses

The School Expense Surprise

Your child hands you a field-trip form. Payment is due Friday, the same day as the electric bill. They’re waiting for an answer, and you’re doing the math. Can you find room for both?

45–54 Debt and priorities

The Bags Are Packed. The Car Isn’t Ready.

You’ve waited months for this break. Now the car needs an overdue oil change, and the trip money is already stretched. Ignoring it could cost more later. What changes so you can still go?

55–60 Preparing for change

A Smaller Income. The Same Life to Pay For.

You’re ready to slow down. Then you compare your future monthly income with today’s bills, and the numbers don’t fit. What would need to change to make room for the life you want?

61–66 Monthly income

It’s the 20th. Your Money Is Almost Gone.

Your monthly payment felt like enough when it arrived. Now groceries are running low, another bill is due, and the next deposit is days away. How could you make the next month end differently?

67–70 Health essentials

A Chance to Win. A Refill You Need.

A few lottery tickets felt like a little hope. Now your prescription is ready, and there’s less money left than you expected. How will you cover the refill, and protect that money next month?
